# StashHive — Competitor Scan and Differentiation Hypotheses

**Checked:** 2026-10-21  
**Status:** Early desk research only. App-store listings are self-published marketing claims; download counts and reviews are incomplete demand signals, not market-share proof.

## What the quick scan indicates

### Screenshot-only organizers are already active

- [Screenshot Organizer & Search — Bramhank](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.bramhank.screenshotbookmark) describes local OCR text search, categories, note capture, reminders, cleanup, and local backup. The listing showed 10+ downloads and a first-release update dated 3 August 2026 when checked.
- [Screenshot Organizer & Search — Gamey Studio](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en&id=com.gameystudio.screenshot.organizer) describes on-device text recognition, automatic screenshot categorization, collection management, duplicate cleanup, and a one-time premium unlock. Its listing showed 1+ downloads and an update dated 29 September 2026 when checked.
- [GYST Screenshot+Link Organiser](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.aaremm.gyst) combines screenshot management, OCR search, stacks, source links, private sharing, and screenshot discovery. Its listing showed 50K+ downloads, a 3.5-star rating, and 164 reviews when checked. Reviews suggested practical needs around smaller grid sizing, bulk selection, viewing details/date-time, renaming, and launching after capture.
- [SnapFind Screenshot Finder](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.alisherlabs.snapfind) markets screenshot categorization, OCR search, duplicates, folders, and on-device privacy.
- [Screenshot Organizer: SnapSort](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_AU&id=com.saad.screenshotorganizer) markets OCR, smart categories, duplicates, favorites, reminders, and export.

## Strategic implication

**Do not position StashHive primarily as an OCR screenshot manager.** That feature set is already represented in Google Play listings. StashHive's current product hypothesis is broader:

> One personal library for the useful digital things users save across apps: links/web pages, images/screenshots, notes/snippets, PDFs and documents—with one metadata search, lightweight organization, and user-controlled backup.

This is a differentiation hypothesis, not a verified market gap yet. Some existing products may support similar mixed content types, and browser-based alternatives may overlap substantially. A broader feature list alone is not a defensible advantage.

## Candidate gaps to validate

1. **One library across multiple item types:** users can save a URL, copied text, screenshot, and PDF together instead of splitting them across browser bookmarks, gallery, notes, and downloads.
2. **Context retained with saved content:** each item can carry the original URL/source, a personal note, tags, collection, and save time.
3. **Local-first but portable:** core use does not require an account; a tested, user-controlled export/restore workflow reduces lock-in and data-loss anxiety.
4. **Search from one place:** reliably search titles, URLs, notes, tags, and any extracted text that is explicitly supported; don't misrepresent an unsupported OCR result.
5. **Lower-friction, calmer UI:** consistent content-type cards, clear bulk organization plan for a later milestone, understandable details, and an obvious way to retrieve old items.

## What must be validated before committing to a differentiator

- Compare direct mixed-content save-and-find apps (not only screenshot apps) and note whether they support local/offline use, backup portability, cross-content search, and web companion access.
- Read recent negative and feature-request reviews from several relevant apps; distinguish repeated pain points from one-off preferences.
- Test prototypes with 5–10 target users. Ask them to save a real sample (link, image, note, file), then retrieve it later without hints.
- Measure first-save success, retrieval success, confusion between bookmark/preview/offline-copy states, and whether users would keep the app on their phone.
